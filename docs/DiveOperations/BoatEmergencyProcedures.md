# Boat Emergency Procedures & Diver Safety Briefing

## Purpose

To standardize how Sea Saba dive staff conduct emergency-related diver briefings and respond to on-site emergencies from company vessels. This SOP ensures consistent, clear communication with guests and a coordinated response between guides, captains, and crew.

## Scope

This SOP applies to all Sea Saba dive guides, instructors, and vessel captains conducting dives from company boats. It covers emergency procedures relevant to boat-based dive operations only. Shore-based emergency procedures and detailed medical response protocols are maintained separately in the manifest box aboard each vessel.

## Guiding Philosophy

Safety briefings should be **simple, clear, and practical**. Guests should leave a briefing feeling informed and confident — not anxious or overwhelmed.

- Divers should understand that if something unusual happens underwater, they should **follow the guide**, who will direct the appropriate response.
- Staff should avoid overloading divers with complex emergency scenarios that may cause confusion or panic in the water.
- Guides and captains are expected to exercise **professional judgment** based on conditions, group experience, and the specific circumstances of each situation.

> The goal is to prepare divers without alarming them. A calm, confident briefing sets the tone for the entire dive.

## Standard Diver Safety Briefing Requirements

Every diver briefing from a Sea Saba vessel must include the following emergency-related points:

1. **Follow the Guide**: In any unusual or unexpected situation underwater, divers should remain with and follow their guide. The guide will assess the situation and direct the group.
2. **Buddy System**: Divers must stay with their buddy at all times. No solo diving.
3. **Air Monitoring**: Signal the guide at half tank. Return to the boat with a minimum of 500 psi / 50 bar.
4. **Separation Protocol**: If a diver becomes separated from the group, follow the procedures outlined in the [Missing Diver Procedures](#missing-diver-procedures) section below.
5. **Surfacing**: Divers should not swim toward the vessel after surfacing unless signaled by the captain. Wait for the boat to come to you.
6. **Current Awareness**: If current is present, stay close to the guide. Controlled group ascents may be conducted away from the mooring if conditions require it.

## Current & Drift Procedures

When current is present or conditions change during a dive:

1. **Stay with the Guide**: Divers must stay close to the guide at all times. Do not drift independently.
2. **SMB Deployment**: The guide may deploy a surface marker buoy (SMB) and conduct a controlled group ascent away from the mooring line if current or conditions make a mooring ascent unsafe.
3. **Stay Together on Ascent**: Divers must remain as a group during ascent and upon surfacing.
4. **Do Not Swim to the Boat**: After surfacing, divers should hold position and wait. **Do not swim toward the vessel.**
5. **Captain Approach**: The captain will maneuver the vessel to the divers and will signal clearly when it is safe to approach and board.

!!! warning
    Divers swimming toward a moving vessel in current or choppy conditions creates significant safety risk. Staff must reinforce this point during every briefing when current is a possibility.

## Emergency Recall Philosophy

Sea Saba does **not** brief the industry-standard emergency recall signal of banging on the ladder with a weight.

!!! note
    Divers regularly hear metallic and percussive sounds underwater — anchor chains, boat engines, and other noise sources are common. Briefing an emergency recall signal based on metallic banging risks triggering unnecessary panic or uncontrolled ascents if divers misidentify a routine sound as an emergency signal.

Instead, divers should be briefed that:

- In unusual situations, they should **follow the guide**, who will assess the situation and direct the group's response.
- The guide is responsible for managing any in-water emergency and will communicate through standard hand signals.

## Missing Diver Procedures

### For Divers

If a diver becomes separated from the group underwater:

1. **Stay Calm**: Do not panic. Separation is manageable if handled correctly.
2. **Search Briefly**: Search for approximately **one minute** while remaining stationary or making a slow, controlled turn. Do not descend further or swim into poor visibility.
3. **Ascend Safely**: If the group is not located, begin a slow and controlled ascent. Monitor above and around for other divers and vessel traffic.
4. **Surface and Signal**: Once on the surface, signal the vessel immediately using your SMB or by waving. Stay visible and hold position.

> This procedure should be included in every diver briefing. Keep the language calm and matter-of-fact — separation is uncommon but manageable when divers know what to do.

### For Guides

If a diver in your group goes missing:

1. **Assess the Situation**: Consider visibility, current strength, the missing diver's experience and gas supply, and the safety of the remaining group.
2. **Brief Search**: Conduct a reasonable search based on conditions and your professional judgment. Do not compromise the safety of the remaining group.
3. **Prioritize Group Safety**: The guide's primary responsibility is the safety of the divers still in their care.
4. **Surface and Report**: If the missing diver is not located within a reasonable search period, surface the group and immediately notify the captain.
5. **Captain Coordinates Recovery**: The captain will begin surface search and initiate emergency contact procedures as needed.

!!! note
    This SOP intentionally avoids prescribing rigid underwater search patterns or time requirements. Conditions vary significantly across dive sites. Guides are expected to apply professional judgment and situational awareness at all times.

## Guide Responsibilities

During any emergency or unusual situation:

- **Maintain group control** — keep the remaining divers calm, together, and safe.
- **Communicate clearly** with hand signals appropriate to the situation.
- **Do not leave the group** to conduct an extended solo search for a missing diver if doing so would compromise the remaining divers' safety.
- **Surface and coordinate** with the captain as quickly as conditions allow when a situation cannot be fully managed underwater.
- **Document** the circumstances of any emergency or incident after the dive.

## Captain Responsibilities

- **Maintain constant awareness** of diver locations, SMB deployments, and expected surfacing areas throughout the dive.
- **Monitor conditions**: Watch for changes in current, weather, or vessel traffic that may affect diver safety.
- **Coordinate pickup safely**: Maneuver the vessel carefully when recovering divers in current or rough conditions. Signal clearly before divers approach the vessel.
- **Avoid unnecessary risk**: Do not create propeller or hull hazards when approaching surfaced divers.
- **Communicate clearly** with guides and crew during any emergency.
- **Initiate emergency response** immediately upon being notified of a missing diver or medical emergency.

## Emergency Departure Procedures

In rare cases, a serious life-threatening emergency on the vessel may require the boat to depart the dive site immediately — for example, to transport a critically injured person to medical care.

**What divers and guides underwater should know:**

- If you hear or sense the boat leaving unexpectedly, **remain calm and continue the dive normally**.
- Return to the mooring line or your planned surfacing location as you would at the end of a normal dive.
- **Stay together** as a group. The guide is responsible for maintaining control of the divers in their care.
- **The vessel will return** — either the same boat or another Sea Saba vessel will come back to recover the group as soon as possible.

!!! danger
    Under no circumstances should divers attempt an emergency ascent or deviate from safe diving practices because the boat has departed. An uncontrolled ascent creates serious decompression risk and does not improve the situation.

> This scenario is extremely rare. Briefing it should be brief and matter-of-fact: "If the boat ever has to leave unexpectedly, continue your dive normally and surface at the mooring. We will come back for you."

## Injury & Medical Emergency Reference

Detailed injury and medical emergency response procedures are located in the **manifest box aboard each vessel**. The manifest box contains:

- Emergency contact numbers
- Coast Guard and local emergency services information
- Step-by-step emergency response procedures
- Passenger and crew manifest

All dive staff are expected to familiarize themselves with the contents of the manifest box regularly and know its location on each vessel before departure.

!!! note
    Do not wait for an emergency to locate the manifest box. Know where it is before every trip.

## Staff Expectations

1. **Brief Consistently**: All dive staff must include the emergency-related points from this SOP in every diver briefing. Consistency builds diver confidence and reduces confusion if something goes wrong.
2. **Stay Calm**: Present emergency information calmly and with confidence. Staff who appear anxious or uncertain will make guests anxious. Set a composed, professional tone.
3. **Prioritize Safety**: In any emergency, the priority order is: diver safety → group control → calm decision-making → communication with the captain.
4. **Use Judgment**: No SOP can account for every possible situation. Trained dive staff are expected to apply professional judgment and act in the best interest of guest safety at all times.
5. **Report Incidents**: Any emergency, near-miss, or unusual incident must be reported to management promptly after the dive.

## Important Notes

- This SOP covers **boat-based operations only**. Shore-based and post-dive emergency procedures are covered separately.
- The **manifest box** on each vessel is the primary reference for medical emergencies and emergency contacts. Know its location.
- Emergency recall by banging on the ladder is **not briefed** to guests. Guides direct the group's response underwater.
- Divers should **never swim toward the vessel** after surfacing unless signaled by the captain.
- The boat departing unexpectedly is **not a signal to ascend immediately** — continue the dive and surface normally.

## Accountability

All dive guides, instructors, and vessel captains are responsible for understanding and following this SOP. Consistent briefings and professional emergency responses are a core expectation of all Sea Saba dive staff.

---

> Emergency situations vary in nature, severity, and circumstance. No written procedure replaces the professional judgment of trained dive staff. Guides and captains are trusted to assess each situation and act accordingly — calmly, safely, and in the best interest of everyone on the water.
