# Vessel Operating Speeds & Harbor Entry

## Purpose

To establish safe and consistent operating expectations for Sea Saba vessel captains regarding cruising RPM, fuel economy, sea condition awareness, and harbor entry procedures. This SOP prioritizes the safety of passengers, crew, and vessels above all other operational considerations.

## Scope

This SOP applies to all Sea Saba captains and crew operating company vessels, including catamarans equipped with Yamaha 300 outboard engines and any other company vessels in regular operation.

## General Vessel Operating Philosophy

Vessel operation at Sea Saba should always prioritize, in order:

1. **Safety** — of passengers, crew, and the vessel itself
2. **Passenger comfort** — smooth, controlled operation in all conditions
3. **Mechanical longevity** — operating within ranges that reduce unnecessary wear
4. **Fuel efficiency** — responsible fuel consumption during normal operations

Captains are expected to apply **professional judgment** based on weather, sea state, visibility, passenger comfort, vessel loading, and operational needs at all times. Safe operation always takes priority over speed or schedule.

> There is no trip, appointment, or timeline that justifies operating a vessel unsafely. If conditions require slowing down or delaying, that is the correct decision.

## Cruising RPM Guidelines

For Sea Saba catamarans equipped with **Yamaha 300 outboard engines**, the general target cruising RPM for normal operations is:

> **~3500 RPM** — target range for fuel economy and comfortable cruising under typical conditions

This is a **guideline**, not a hard restriction. Captains should understand:

- **Exceeding 3500 RPM** is acceptable when conditions, safety considerations, currents, timing, or vessel handling require it.
- **Operating below 3500 RPM** may be appropriate in rough seas, confined areas, or when passenger comfort requires a slower pace.
- Different sea states, wave intervals, and passenger or fuel loads will affect how the vessel performs at any given RPM.
- The correct cruising speed is the one that provides the **safest and smoothest ride for the conditions** — not necessarily a fixed number.

!!! note
    RPM guidelines are provided as a starting point for fuel-efficient operation. Captains should prioritize vessel handling and passenger safety over adherence to any specific RPM target.

## Sea Conditions & Operator Judgment

Vessel performance varies significantly based on conditions. Captains must continuously adjust operating speed based on:

- **Wave interval and direction**: Short, steep chop may require slowing down. Long swells may be cut more effectively at moderate speed.
- **Wind conditions**: Headwinds and beam seas affect vessel stability and passenger comfort at different speeds.
- **Passenger and equipment loading**: Heavier loads change vessel response and may require speed adjustments.
- **Fuel load**: A full fuel load affects draft and handling characteristics.
- **Current and sea state**: Strong currents may require increased throttle to maintain control or reach a destination safely.

Captains are expected to **reduce slamming, improve control, and maximize passenger comfort** by adjusting speed proactively throughout each trip — not just at departure and arrival.

## Harbor Entry Procedures

Harbor approaches must always be **slow, controlled, and deliberate**.

1. **Reduce Speed Early**: Begin reducing speed well before entering the harbor area. Do not wait until the dock is visible to slow down.
2. **Controlled Approach**: Enter the harbor at a speed that gives full control over the vessel at all times.
3. **Maintain Reaction Distance**: Always maintain enough distance, speed margin, and reaction time to safely stop or maneuver if a mechanical issue occurs.
4. **Assess Before Entering**: Before committing to the harbor approach, assess vessel traffic, docking space, wind direction, and current. If conditions are not favorable, slow down further or hold position until they are.
5. **Final Approach**: The final approach to the dock should be at **minimum speed** — just enough to maintain steerage and control.

!!! danger
    Never approach the harbor or dock at excessive speed. There is no safe recovery from a high-speed approach if something goes wrong.

## Throttle Failure Awareness

Captains must always operate with the awareness that **throttle or shift controls could fail at any moment**. This is not a hypothetical concern — mechanical failures do occur.

The critical rule is:

> **Never place the vessel in a position where a throttle malfunction could immediately result in a collision.**

If a vessel enters a harbor at excessive speed and the throttle fails to respond:

- The vessel will continue at full speed toward the dock, shoreline, or other boats.
- There may be no time or space to correct course.

The potential consequences of such an incident include:

- **Serious injury or death** to passengers, crew, or bystanders
- **Severe vessel damage** or total loss
- **Damage to harbor infrastructure**, docks, or other boats
- **Significant legal and financial liability**

!!! danger
    Slow harbor entries are not optional or a matter of preference — they are a mandatory safety requirement. A controlled entry eliminates the scenario where throttle failure becomes catastrophic.

## Safety Expectations

- There is **no operational benefit** worth risking an accident during harbor entry.
- **Conservative operation is the standard**, not the exception. Captains who operate carefully and patiently are operating correctly.
- Management fully supports captains who slow down, hold position, or take extra time when conditions or safety require it.
- If another party — guest, staff, or otherwise — pressures a captain to operate unsafely or rush a harbor approach, the captain has full authority and responsibility to disregard that pressure.

## Captain Responsibilities

1. **Prioritize safety** over speed, convenience, or schedule at all times.
2. **Operate conservatively** in confined spaces, harbors, and around other vessels.
3. **Monitor vessel response** continuously while maneuvering — throttle response, steering feel, and engine behavior.
4. **Maintain situational awareness** around docks, swimmers, divers on the surface, moored vessels, and harbor traffic.
5. **Secure passengers** — ensure all passengers are safely seated and aware of the vessel's movements during harbor maneuvers.
6. **Report mechanical concerns** immediately. Any unusual throttle, steering, or engine behavior should be reported to management before the vessel's next operation.

## Important Notes

- **~3500 RPM** is a cruising guideline for Yamaha 300-equipped catamarans — adjust as conditions require.
- Exceeding this RPM is acceptable when safety, conditions, or handling demand it.
- Harbor entries must always be **slow, controlled, and deliberate** — no exceptions.
- Throttle failure at high speed inside a harbor is a **life-threatening scenario**. Operating at low speed eliminates this risk.
- Conservative operation is encouraged and supported by management.
- Any mechanical concerns with throttle, shift, or engine response must be reported before the vessel continues operations.

## Accountability

All Sea Saba captains are responsible for operating company vessels safely and in accordance with this SOP. Management is responsible for supporting captains who make conservative, safety-first operational decisions.

---

> Professional judgment, patience, and conservative operation are not signs of inexperience — they are the hallmarks of a skilled captain. Take your time, operate carefully, and never place the vessel or its passengers in an avoidable risk.
